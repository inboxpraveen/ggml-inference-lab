Step 3, continued pretraining, second half: what the knobs do, and the main run

Four hundred steps is {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_TOKENS_M}}M tokens and about ten minutes, which is cheap enough to ask the recipe's questions one at a time. Each ablation below is a single run with everything held fixed except the one thing named, all from the same shuffled data order, all scored on the same held-out documents. The vocabulary is the first question, because it changes what a step means.

Read the left panel first, because it says something I didn't expect. Per character of Hindi seen, the stock tokenizer learns faster: by {{ABL_STOCK_HI_CHARS_M}}M characters it's at {{RUN_ABL_STOCK_BPC_HI}} bits per character and the extended tokenizer at that point is still at {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC100_HI}}. That's not a paradox. Under the stock tokenizer a character is four tokens, so every character gets four forward passes and four gradient updates, and the model already knows its byte tokens. The extended tokenizer is spending its first hundred steps learning what {{NEW_TOKENS_16K}} new rows mean. But nobody trains by the character; they train by the step, and the right panel is the same runs in minutes. There the extended tokenizer passes the stock one inside three minutes and ends the 400 steps at {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC_HI}} against {{RUN_ABL_STOCK_BPC_HI}}, having seen {{ABL_16K_HI_CHARS_M}}M characters to the stock run's {{ABL_STOCK_HI_CHARS_M}}M in the same time. And that's the training side only. At inference the extended model reads and writes Hindi in {{TPW_GAIN_16K}}x fewer tokens forever, which on the bandwidth arithmetic of the first three posts is {{TPW_GAIN_16K}}x the words per second and {{TPW_GAIN_16K}}x the context. The three extended sizes end within a few hundredths of each other ({{RUN_ABL_8K_BPC_HI}}, {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC_HI}} and {{RUN_ABL_32K_BPC_HI}} for 8k, 16k and 29k added tokens), which says the vocabulary size is a fertility decision, not a quality one, at least at this budget; more rows are more parameters to train and the extra rows are the rarest words.

This is the ablation whose result I'd have bet on and still found useful to see. With no English in the mix, Hindi reaches {{RUN_ABL_NOREPLAY_BPC_HI}} bits per character after 400 steps, the best of the three by a hair, and English goes from {{BPC_STOCK_EN}} to {{RUN_ABL_NOREPLAY_BPC_EN}}, which is a {{NOREPLAY_EN_DRIFT_PCT}}% loss on text from the model's own pretraining distribution, in nine minutes. Ten percent English costs {{ABL_NOREPLAY_HI_GAIN_VS_10}} bits of Hindi and holds English to {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC_EN}}; thirty percent costs {{ABL_REPLAY30_HI_LOSS_VS_10}} more bits of Hindi and holds it to {{RUN_ABL_REPLAY30_BPC_EN}}. None of them holds English still, and none of them should be expected to: the model is being asked to fit a second language into the same 135M parameters, and something has to move. Ibrahim and colleagues' {{SRC_IBRAHIM_CPT_REPLAY_STRONG_SHIFT_PCT}}% for a strong shift and Swallow's {{SRC_SWALLOW_JA_EN_RATIO_USED}} are the two ends of the published range; I used ten for the main run because Hindi is the point and the English loss at ten is one I'd take, and because the replay share is the one knob you can revisit later without retraining anything else.

The learning rate buys Hindi with English. At a peak of 2e-4 the model reaches {{RUN_ABL_LR2E_4_BPC_HI}} bits per character of Hindi and keeps English at {{RUN_ABL_LR2E_4_BPC_EN}}; at 5e-4, {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC_HI}} and {{RUN_ABL_16K_MEAN_BPC_EN}}; at 1e-3, {{RUN_ABL_LR1E_3_BPC_HI}} and {{RUN_ABL_LR1E_3_BPC_EN}}, which is the best Hindi of any 400-step run in this post and the worst English, a {{LR1E3_EN_DRIFT_PCT}}% loss. There's no free point on that line; the replay share and the learning rate are two views of the same trade. I took 5e-4 for the main run as the middle of the range, with the note that a longer run spends most of its time below the peak anyway, so the choice matters less than the ablation makes it look. The one setting I'd call wrong is the lowest: at 2e-4 the new embedding rows are still visibly behind after 400 steps, because {{NEW_TOKENS_16K}} rows that start as averages need to move a long way and a small step takes longer to get them there.

The main run

With those settled (16k tokens, mean initialisation, 10% English, peak {{RUN_MAIN_LR}}), the main run is {{RUN_MAIN_STEPS}} steps: {{RUN_MAIN_TOKENS_M}}M tokens, of which {{RUN_MAIN_HI_TOKENS_M}}M are Hindi, which is {{RUN_MAIN_HI_CHARS_M}}M characters of Hindi text, in {{RUN_MAIN_MIN}} minutes on the laptop. For scale, that's about {{MAIN_SHARE_OF_FETCHED_PCT}}% of the Hindi I downloaded, which was itself under one percent of the FineWeb-2 Hindi subset, which is one of {{SRC_FINEWEB2_LANGUAGE_SCRIPT_PAIRS}} language subsets in a corpus that is itself a filtered slice of the web.

Hindi goes from {{BPC_STOCK_HI}} bits per character (stock tokenizer) through {{RUN_MAIN_BPC0_HI}} (the moment the new rows go in) to {{RUN_MAIN_BPC_HI}} at the end, {{BPC_MAIN_HI}} on the larger {{EVAL_DOCS}}-document set the size chart uses. In that chart the adapted 135M now sits below every stock model except the one built for the language: {{BPC_QWEN3_17_HI}} for Qwen3-1.7B, {{BPC_QWEN15_HI}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B, {{BPC_17B_HI}} for SmolLM2-1.7B, twelve times its size, and {{BPC_SARVAM_HI}} for Sarvam-1, which is still a long way ahead. The 1,200-step run had already crossed all three of the larger stock models at {{RUN_RUN1200_BPC_HI}}; the main run's extra {{RUN_MAIN_MIN}} minutes bought the rest. The shape of the curve is worth reading too: the last thousand steps take Hindi from {{MAIN_BPC_HI_2000}} to {{RUN_MAIN_BPC_HI}}, which is the cosine schedule running out of learning rate, not the model running out of things to learn.

The English column is the part I said I'd state plainly. It moves from {{RUN_MAIN_BPC0_EN}} to {{RUN_MAIN_BPC_EN}} bits per character, a {{MAIN_EN_DRIFT_PCT}}% loss on the model's own pretraining distribution, and most of it is in the first thousand steps; after that the curve is flat, because the learning rate has decayed to where it can't move much either way. Ten percent replay slowed the forgetting, it didn't stop it, and at this run length that is the price of the Hindi number above. The replay ablation says what the alternative costs: thirty percent held English at {{RUN_ABL_REPLAY30_BPC_EN}} over 400 steps for {{ABL_REPLAY30_HI_LOSS_VS_10}} bits of Hindi. If the English matters for your use, that's the knob, and it's the one that doesn't require retraining anything else. I kept ten for the main run so that the three runs in the next chart are the same recipe at three lengths.

One objection to that chart is fair and I want to meet it before the scaling section, not after. The held-out Hindi documents are from FineWeb-2, the same crawl the training text came from, and the stock models never saw that distribution. A model that has learned the style of one crawl would look better on that crawl than it deserves. So here are the same models on a second set that nothing in this post trains on: {{WIKI_DOCS}} Hindi Wikipedia articles, a fixed random sample from the November 2023 dump, encyclopedic register rather than web pages.

The objection is half right, and I'm glad I ran it. On Wikipedia the adapted 135M scores {{WIKI_MAIN_HI}} bits per character, not {{BPC_MAIN_HI}}: a third of a bit of the FineWeb-2 result was the model knowing the crawl's register, and the stock models, which never saw that register, lose nothing in the move ({{WIKI_17B_HI}} for SmolLM2-1.7B against {{BPC_17B_HI}}, {{WIKI_QWEN15_HI}} for Qwen2.5-1.5B against {{BPC_QWEN15_HI}}). The ordering survives, just barely: on text it never trained on, the 135M is still under Qwen3-1.7B ({{WIKI_QWEN3_17_HI}}), under SmolLM2-1.7B ({{WIKI_17B_HI}}) and a hundredth or two under Qwen2.5-1.5B ({{WIKI_QWEN15_HI}}), while Sarvam-1 sits at {{BPC_SARVAM_HI}} and {{WIKI_SARVAM_HI}}. The 1,200-step run does not survive it: at {{WIKI_RUN1200_HI}} on Wikipedia it's level with the 1.7B models it had beaten by a third of a bit on the crawl. So the sentence I can defend is narrower than the first chart suggests, and it's this: after {{RUN_MAIN_MIN}} minutes on a laptop, a 135M model matches or edges the best general-purpose stock models under 2B on Hindi text from a source it never saw, and is a long way behind the one model that was built for the language. Every Hindi number after this point is quoted on both sets where I have both.

If we keep going

The curve above is one run, and the shape of its last third is the learning rate decaying, not the data running out; a single cosine schedule can't tell you what more data would do. The honest version of "what if we continued" is to run the whole schedule at several budgets and look at where each one ends. I have three: the 400-step ablation, a 1,200-step run, and the main run, each with its own warmup and decay, each ending at a different number of Hindi tokens.

The three points sit close to a straight line on log-log axes, and the line's slope is {{SCALE_EXP}}: every doubling of Hindi tokens takes about {{SCALE_PER_DOUBLING_PCT}} percent off the bits per character, for now. Read the horizontal lines against it. The 400-step run, ten minutes, already sits under SmolLM2-360M; the 1,200-step run is under every non-Indic stock model on the chart, including the two 1.7B models (on this set; on Wikipedia it is level with them); and the main run is clear of them on both. The line reaches Sarvam-1's {{BPC_SARVAM_HI}} at about {{SCALE_TOKENS_TO_SARVAM}} Hindi tokens, roughly four times the main run, and I want to be careful with that number. A power law fitted to three points will fit almost anything, and these curves bend: the returns from the same data source flatten as the model learns what that source has to teach, and a 135M model has a floor that a 2B model trained on {{SRC_SARVAM1_INDIC_TOKENS_SHORT}} tokens of Indic text does not. What the chart does support is the weaker claim, which is the one the post set out to test: the recipe keeps paying as it's extended, the rate of payment hasn't started to fall inside the range I could afford, and the larger general-purpose stock models are already behind. Getting near Sarvam-1 would be an experiment, not an extrapolation.
